03

Flippers — new wiring via the Cobra board

Decision: route the flipper switch + coil through Cobra chain 0 (chain0-0x20, port COM4) instead of the PSOC chain. CobraPin's own documentation says timing-critical devices (flippers, slings, pop bumpers) should have their switch and coil on the same physical STM32 board, so the hardware autofire rule runs with minimal latency and doesn't need to cross a board boundary. Chain 0 has plenty of free capacity (14 of 16 solenoid outputs, 13 of 15 switch inputs after this) and already anchors most of the game's LEDs, so it's the natural home. Numbers below are read directly off board Overviews.xlsx's Cobrapin - Game Funtion pin dump.

Coil bank — connector pins + which lug goes where

One coil lug lands on the +50V supply bus (VIN terminal block) — never the board. The other lug lands on the board's driver pin (0-0-2/0-0-3), which is a ground-sink: the on-board MOSFET pulls it to ground to fire the coil, it does not supply power itself. A 4004-type flyback diode goes in parallel across the coil, cathode band toward the +50V lug — without it, the field collapse can destroy the driver MOSFET (per the xlsx Coil sheet).

Switch wing — connector pins + which lug goes where

One switch lug lands on the board's input pin (0-0-9/0-0-10) — already pulled up to 5V inside the board, so never wire external power to it. The other lug lands on the logic ground bus (the board's plain "GND" pin — a separate rail from the "GND(50V)" and "GND(LED)" pins the coil/LED supplies return to). Closing the switch grounds the pin, which reads as "active" (per the xlsx Switch sheet).
Before energizing anything: pin positions above are read off the xlsx silkscreen labels, not yet confirmed with a meter on the real board. Do a physical continuity check from each terminal back to its labeled address before landing a wire, exactly like every other DRAFT number in TODO.md. The board exposes at least three separate ground rails (logic GND, GND(50V), GND(LED)) — tie them all together at the power supply, don't assume they're the same physical terminal. Bench-test each flipper at low power (short pulse, held by hand) before a full unrestrained swing — listen for the mechanical EOS interrupter cutting hold current cleanly.
Bench Test Procedure
04

Before power-on

Visual/mechanical pass with the machine unplugged. Catches the cheap failures before anything gets energized.

No loose wire ends, pinched harnesses, or connectors sitting half-seated on any of the three OPP chains (COM4/COM5 Cobra, COM6 PSOC ×4)
Trough holds exactly 4 balls (matches balls_installed: 4 in hardware-basic.yaml) — add/remove before powering on, not after
Flipper mechs: dual-wound coils seated, mechanical EOS interrupter moves freely by hand, no binding at end of stroke
Drop target bank (3 targets) resets freely by hand, no snagging in the guide rails
Sling and popbumper rubber in good condition, not so loose/tight it changes the expected pulse strength

Ports seen:

Firmware versions match the reference scan (0x2040000 on chain 0/1, 0x1000004 on chain 2 boards)
MPF boots to attract mode with no config warnings/errors in the console

LED check

No dead/unlit pixels anywhere in the 0-0-0…39 run
