03

Flippers — new wiring via the Cobra board

Decision: route the flipper switch + coil through Cobra chain 0 (chain0-0x20, port COM4) instead of the PSOC chain. CobraPin's own documentation says timing-critical devices (flippers, slings, pop bumpers) should have their switch and coil on the same physical STM32 board, so the hardware autofire rule runs with minimal latency and doesn't need to cross a board boundary. Chain 0 has plenty of free capacity (14 of 16 solenoid outputs, 13 of 15 switch inputs after this) and already anchors most of the game's LEDs, so it's the natural home. Coil bank sketch — chain 0, driver connector

Coil lugs run positive from the high-voltage supply; the driver pin sinks to ground when fired. A 4004-type flyback diode is required across every coil (band toward the positive lead) — without it the collapsing field can destroy the driver MOSFET.

Switch wing sketch — chain 0, input connector

Switch pins idle at 5V (pull-up); the cabinet button grounds the pin to register "closed." Runs to logic ground, not the high-voltage supply — keep this return separate from the coil's ground return.
Before energizing anything: pin positions above are read off the xlsx silkscreen labels, not yet confirmed with a meter on the real board. Do a physical continuity check from each terminal back to its labeled address before landing a wire, exactly like every other DRAFT number in TODO.md. Tie the flipper coil's ground return to the same power-supply ground as everything else on the board (no floating grounds), and bench-test each flipper at low power (short pulse, held by hand) before a full unrestrained swing — listen for the mechanical EOS interrupter cutting hold current cleanly.
