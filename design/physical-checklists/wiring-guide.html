Board map

Six physical CPU boards across three USB-serial chains. The two Cobra boards share one physical PCB (two STM32 modules) but count as independent CPUs/chains for wiring purposes — a hardware autofire rule cannot cross between them, or onto the PSOC chain. This machine is being rewired from scratch, board assignment follows a fixed priority: CobraPin first (chain 0, then chain 1), the red PSOC boards (chain 2) only as a fallback when CobraPin can't fit something, and every LED goes on CobraPin, no exceptions. See docs/opp-hardware-reference.md for the full rulebook this page is built from. CobraPin banks & HV power reference

The 3 solenoid banks (A/B/C, 8 outputs each) each have their own fused HV supply pin (HV_A/HV_B/HV_C on connector J13) — a coil's +50V lead must come from the HV pin matching its own bank, never a generic shared bus. Full detail and sources in docs/opp-hardware-reference.md.

BankChain 0/1 pinsHV feedWire color
A0-0-0, 0-0-8…0-0-14HV_Abrown — includes flipper coils 0-0-8/0-0-9, slings 0-0-0/0-0-12, ball saver 0-0-13
B0-0-1…0-0-7, 0-0-15HV_Bteal
C1-0-0…1-0-7HV_Cpurple
Mixing up bank power and bank driver can blow FETs, coils, and fuses (CobraPin's own doc wording). Test without coil power first, and check the per-bank yellow coil LED to confirm a bank actually has power before trusting it. A bank's connector pins can also be pre-broken-out to a wall-mounted terminal block without any coil actually being wired to them yet — wires present on a bank's connector doesn't by itself mean it's in use, check the far end of the run. tools/wiring_test.py automates the switch-continuity + coil-LED half of this check (see the repo README) — used first for the flippers, 2026-09-06.
03

Red boards — classic/modular OPP (chain 2)

Chain 2's 4 PSOC boards follow a different, tighter rule than CobraPin — used here only as the fallback when CobraPin can't fit a component (per section 01's priority). Full detail in docs/opp-hardware-reference.md.

Up to 4 plug-in "wings" per board (solenoid/switch/lamp, mixed freely) — not one combo PCB like CobraPin. 5V switch logic, vs. CobraPin's 3.3V.
Numbering is wing-position-based: switches = wing×8 (0-7, 8-15, 16-23, 24-31), coils = wing×4 (0-3, 4-7, 8-11, 12-15).
Pairing rule: a solenoid wing's own dedicated switch inputs are the first four switch numbers of that same wing — a coil in wing N (4N..4N+3) pairs only with a switch in 8N..8N+3, not just "anywhere on the same board." Confirmed against this cabinet's own working hardware: c-sling-right/left (2-0-0/1, since moved to the Cobra board — see section 04's table) paired with switches 2-0-0/1; c-popbumper-1/2/3 (2-0-4/5/6) pair with switches 2-0-8/9/10 — the first 3 of wing 1's 4 dedicated slots.

Flippers — new wiring via the Cobra board

Decision: route the flipper switch + coil through Cobra chain 0 (chain0-0x20, port COM4) instead of the PSOC chain. CobraPin's own documentation says timing-critical devices (flippers, slings, pop bumpers) should have their switch and coil on the same physical STM32 board, so the hardware autofire rule runs with minimal latency and doesn't need to cross a board boundary. Chain 0 has plenty of free capacity and already anchors most of the game's LEDs, so it's the natural home. Coil numbers (0-0-8/0-0-9) and switch numbers (0-0-1/0-0-2) are both the actual pins used for real physical wiring (2026-09-06, confirmed while wiring); bank/HV feed from section 02's reference table.

Switch wing — connector pins + which lug goes where

One switch lug lands on the board's input pin (0-0-1/0-0-2) — already pulled up to 3.3V inside the board (CobraPin's STM32 logic level — the red/PSOC boards elsewhere in this cabinet are 5V, see section 03), so never wire external power to it. The other lug lands on the logic ground bus (the board's plain "GND" pin — a separate rail from the "GND(50V)" and "GND(LED)" pins the coil/LED supplies return to). Closing the switch grounds the pin, which reads as "active" (per the xlsx Switch sheet).
Before energizing anything: the switch numbers (0-0-1/0-0-2) and coil numbers (0-0-8/0-0-9) above are the actual pins used for real wiring, confirmed by the user while wiring (2026-09-06) — but do a physical continuity check from each terminal back to its labeled address before energizing, per the open item in TODO.md. The board exposes at least three separate ground rails (logic GND, GND(50V), GND(LED)) — tie them all together at the power supply, don't assume they're the same physical terminal. A 4004-type flyback diode goes in parallel across each coil, cathode band toward the HV_A lug — without it, the field collapse can destroy the driver MOSFET (per the xlsx Coil sheet). Double-check the flipper coils' +50V leads land on HV_A specifically (brown) — landing on HV_B or HV_C by mistake risks a blown FET/fuse per section 02. Watch Bank A's yellow coil LED to confirm it actually has power before the first real fire. Bench-test each flipper at low power (short pulse, held by hand) before a full unrestrained swing — listen for the mechanical EOS interrupter cutting hold current cleanly.
